September in Baton Rouge is a negotiation. The heat still owns the afternoon, but the evenings start giving ground — a few honest minutes of dark on the back end of every drive. Here’s what’s news.

On the Road

i3 Touring prototype · Nürburgring · Carscoops / Baldauf

The wagon nobody was supposed to build

Somewhere at the Nürburgring last month, a silver estate went out for laps under a thin coat of camouflage, and the shutter caught it. Carscoops ran the photos on 19 August. Credit to them and to Baldauf.

What he caught is the i3 Touring — the long-roof version of BMW’s Neue Klasse i3 sedan. A driver’s-shape electric wagon, which is not a phrase the market has heard in a long time. The prototype doing the work is the dual-motor 50 xDrive: 463 horsepower, a 108.7 kWh battery, roughly 550 miles on the European cycle, and 400 kW charging that turns a stop into a coffee. Munich already started building the sedan this month.

Two corrections before anyone gets ahead of it. It is not a hybrid. And a car turning laps in Germany is proof that engineers are busy, not that a Baton Rouge delivery date exists. Nobody has promised us this car. A long-roof that charges and actually corners is the argument against the idea that every good answer has to be a taller SUV.

On the Table

Tsunami Downtown · Shaw Center rooftop · Photos: Tsunami

Eat up high, then take the stairs

Six floors over the Mississippi, on the roof of the Shaw Center, there’s a 4,000-square-foot terrace that spends most of the year too hot to bother with. September is when it pays off. Eat up here, then walk downstairs to Manship. Same building. No parking twice.

Start with the yuzu albacore — thin sashimi, a hit of citrus. Then the rolls: the Big Easy (crawfish, snow crab, fried shrimp, soy wrap, panko-fried, $18), the Black & Gold (crunchy shrimp, Kani, crawfish, baked spicy mayo, $18), the Hung Lo (panko shrimp, seared tuna, snow crab, $20). Two people. The last of the daylight.

Closed Sunday and Monday. Tuesday through Thursday, 11 to 9. If you’re headed downstairs at 7:30, book the earlier seating and mean it.

On the Town

Jason Carter Band · Manship Theatre · 24 September · Photo: Michael Weintrob

Jason Carter Band, September 24

Downstairs from that rooftop, Jason Carter spent years as the right hand of the Del McCoury Band and is out front now, singing lead on a forthcoming solo record, Lowdown Hoedown. Manship Theatre, 7:30 to 9:30, from $38. Modern, muscular Americana — Asleep at the Wheel, Steve Earle, Ricky Skaggs in the family tree. Fast when it needs to be, quiet when it means it.

Buy from the theatre. Every patron needs a ticket. Third-party sellers are a tax you don’t have to pay.

On the Wrist

Black Bay 58 · ref. 7939A1A0NU · Hodinkee / Tudor

Tudor fixed the one thing

The Black Bay 58 was already the smart dive watch — 39 mm, the right size. This spring at Watches & Wonders, Tudor updated it. The number that matters: 11.7 mm thick, down from 11.9. Still 200 meters. Still a proper unidirectional 60-minute bezel. Black matte dial, gently domed, text trimmed to two lines, hands drawn slimmer. Grade A Super-LumiNova.

Under the caseback: Caliber MT5400-U, silicon hairspring, 65-hour reserve, COSC and METAS. Master Chronometer. Zero to plus-five seconds a day. The one to want is the black dial on the new five-link bracelet with the T-fit clasp: $5,350. Three-link $5,225, rubber $4,975. Available now. Not a lottery.
