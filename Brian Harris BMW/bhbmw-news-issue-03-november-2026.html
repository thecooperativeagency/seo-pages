The All-New X5

Meet the Fifth-Generation X5

In 1999 the X5 created the Sports Activity Vehicle. The fifth generation brings what BMW calls "the broadest range of drive systems ever offered in a BMW model." It's still built at BMW Plant Spartanburg, South Carolina, the Home of X.

The X5 40 xDrive leads off. It went on sale in October with an updated 3.0-liter inline-six and a 48-volt mild hybrid. It makes 394 horsepower, 19 more than before, and 428 lb-ft of torque, and BMW quotes 0–60 mph in 5.1 seconds.

Three more arrive in the first quarter of 2027. The rear-wheel-drive X5 40. The X5 50e xDrive plug-in hybrid, with 483 horsepower of system output and up to 44 miles on electricity (BMW preliminary estimate). And the first all-electric X5, the iX5 60 xDrive. A V8 M Performance model is scheduled for later in 2027.

The iX5 is the quick one. It has up to 570 horsepower, an estimated 0–60 of 4.4 seconds, 800-volt technology and an estimated 435 miles of range (preliminary BMW AG estimate using EPA procedures). Every X5 also gets BMW Panoramic Vision, which runs the key information in a band across the base of the windshield.

BMW USA's published base MSRPs are $69,800 for the X5 40, $72,100 for the X5 40 xDrive, $77,500 for the X5 50e xDrive and $79,800 for the iX5 60 xDrive. Each adds $1,450 destination and handling. These are BMW's prices, not a Brian Harris BMW offer. Ask the team on Airline Highway which X5 fits your drive.

This month's X5 offers are on the 2026 BMW X5, not the all-new model. You'll find them in On the Lot below.

On the Road

The iX3 M60 xDrive, 603 Horsepower

BMW iX3 M60 xDrive · BMW PressClub USA

On the Road

The iX3 M60 xDrive, 603 Horsepower

The iX3 now gets the version a driver actually asks about. The iX3 M60 xDrive arrives at 603 horsepower and 645 lb-ft. Zero to 62 in 3.9 seconds, per BMW. That's a Sports Activity Vehicle (SAV) doing sports-car arithmetic, quietly, on the way to Perkins Rowe.

The part that matters is how it's tuned. BMW's "Heart of Joy" controller and M Dynamic Performance Control sort out the drive in milliseconds. Paddles on the wheel change how hard M eDrive answers. Sound and feedback scale with speed, because an M car is supposed to talk to you, battery or not.

Production starts at BMW's Debrecen plant in Hungary in spring 2027. U.S. pricing isn't out yet. When it is, it runs here.

On the Table

Joelle's, Soul Food Dressed Up

Joelle's Soul and Savour · 3015 Westfork Dr.

On the Table

Joelle's, Soul Food Dressed Up

Joelle's, on Westfork Drive, runs on a simple idea: soul food is worth dressing up for. Southern bones, a hard Caribbean streak, cocktails with intent, a courtyard with live music. Nobody's whispering here. Good.

Order the Velvet Braised Oxtail — Jamaican-style, over white rice, cabbage on the side. It's the dish that tells you whether a kitchen has patience. Then the Jerk Grilled Lamb Chops with a pineapple-mango honey glaze, and the Crawfish Boudin Dip for the table, because this is still Baton Rouge. Finish with the rum-infused bread pudding. At the bar, the Smoke Reserved — Buffalo Trace, finished over smoked cherry wood.

Dinner Tuesday through Saturday, brunch on weekends, closed Mondays. Reservations online.

In the Cup

Magpie Makes Its Own Pop-Tarts

Magpie sits on Perkins by the Overpass, the part of town that still feels like a neighborhood. The kitchen makes its own Pop-Tarts — three days, dough to icing — and this time of year they come in pumpkin spice, apple and brown sugar cinnamon.

The order is the crème brûlée latte, the new one on the fall list. Sweet, burnt-sugar edge, espresso underneath. Grab a Pop-Tart, sit for ten minutes, then point the car down Perkins. The fall menu runs through November.

On the Wrist

The Angelus Chronographe Tachymètre

Angelus Chronographe Tachymètre · Hodinkee

On the Wrist

The Angelus Chronographe Tachymètre

A tachymeter is a speed scale. Time a car over a known distance and read your average off the bezel. It's a driver's complication, and Angelus just built a small, serious one. The Chronographe Tachymètre is 37mm across and 9.25mm thick, a midcentury racing dial with contrasting subdials, on calfskin.

Inside is the hand-wound A5000 monopusher by La Joux-Perret: one button for start, stop and reset, 42 hours of reserve. Three limited editions — 50 in steel with a grey dial and blue accents, 30 in steel with salmon, 15 in yellow gold. Steel is 18,500 Swiss francs (CHF); gold is CHF 34,600, both including Swiss VAT. Small numbers. You won't see one at the next tailgate.

On Heritage

The M1: 277 Horsepower, 1.14 Meters Tall

BMW M1 (E26) · 1978

On Heritage

The M1: 277 Horsepower, 1.14 Meters Tall

Before the M3, before the M5, there was a wedge. The 1978 BMW M1 was the first car BMW Motorsport GmbH designed itself — mid-engine, drawn by Giorgio Giugiaro off Paul Bracq's 1972 Turbo study, and built by hand. Just 460 were made.

The numbers still land. A 3.5-liter inline-six with four valves per cylinder and mechanical injection: 277 hp, about 265 km/h flat out. Curb weight around 1,300 kg. Roof at 1.14 meters. The rule changes that kept it out of its intended series gave it the Procar championship instead — Formula 1 drivers in identical M1s, Niki Lauda champion in 1979, Nelson Piquet in 1980. Andy Warhol painted one, and it ran Le Mans.

And the engine, the M88, went on to power the M635i and the very first M5. Every M car on Airline Highway goes back to this one.
