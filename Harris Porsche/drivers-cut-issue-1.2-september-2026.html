On Saturday

LSU 2–0, and Oxford is waiting

Two games, two beatings. Clemson left Tiger Stadium on the wrong end of 51–10. Louisiana Tech went home 45–14. That’s how you open a season and that’s how Lane Kiffin, in his first fall running LSU, gets a top-ten team and the benefit of nobody’s doubt just yet.

Because the doubt lives in Oxford. This Saturday, September 19, the Tigers go on the road to No. 8 Ole Miss — 7:30 kickoff, the first real weight of the year, and the first time we find out whether the September was scheduling or something meaner. Get through that and it doesn’t get easier: No. 9 Texas A&M comes to Tiger Stadium the following Saturday, September 26, also 7:30, with actual rivalry teeth in it. Two ranked opponents in eight days. That’s the fight.

Southern University

Across town at Ace W. Mumford, the Jaguars are 1–2 and playing the week that still counts as local. This Saturday’s opponent is Louisiana Christian — home, 7:00 p.m. — the kind of date you show up for before the SWAC road gets loud. Next week they go to Jackson State. Homecoming is October 3 against Arkansas–Pine Bluff. Same city, same Saturday calendar. LSU owns the national TV; Southern owns the neighborhood.

On the Road

105 colors, and doors that open themselves

Cayenne Electric · model year · Porsche

105 colors, and doors that open themselves

Porsche put out the model-year update on the Cayenne Electric on September 16, and it reads less like a spec bump than a dare to specify one exactly the way you want it. Paint to Sample lands on the car, which pushes the palette to 105 shades — thirteen standard, and then the long tail where the money and the imagination go. Delgada Green stitching if you’re feeling it. An extended interior style package if you’re feeling it more.

Two things are worth stopping on. First: electrically operated doors, all four, a Porsche first — sensors, and it’ll close itself off the Digital Key, the seatbelt, or the brake pedal. Gimmick or grace note, you decide, but it’s new. Second, and more useful: Porsche Wireless Charging, up to 11 kW at roughly 90 percent efficiency, a pad on the car meeting a pad on the ground, no cable, no ritual. It’s rolling out in stages — orderable in Europe first — but it’s real hardware, not a slide.

Then the theater: massage seats paired with 4D audio — sixteen air cushions and six actuators per front seat, five programs. It applies to the Cayenne Electric SUV and the Coupé both. None of it is on the Macan executive demo upstairs. That’s fine. Different appetite.

On the Table

Vals on Perkins

Vals · 7242 Perkins Road

Vals on Perkins

The pink building on Perkins used to be Rock Paper Taco. Now it’s Vals — the New Orleans taco house that opened its first Baton Rouge room this summer and does not want to be filed under Tex-Mex. Traditional Mexican cooking, a horseshoe bar, $5 tacos on the board, and margaritas squeezed like they mean it.

Come for the elotes, the shrimp quesadilla, the sweet potato or cauliflower taco if you’re ordering for the table. Stay for the bar — the partners also run Cure in New Orleans, so the drink half of the room is not an afterthought. Happy hour daily, 4 to 6. Casual, loud enough, no dress code.

On the Wrist

The steel Daytona in polar white enamel

Rolex Daytona · Watches & Wonders 2026

The steel Daytona in polar white enamel

Rolex spent this year’s Watches & Wonders quietly making the steel Daytona harder to walk past. The headline is the dial: grand feu enamel, all white — the polar reading of the old panda — fired glass rather than paint, which is why it’ll look exactly like this in forty years while lacquer yellows and cracks. It’s the kind of restraint that costs more than flash.

Then Rolex did the thing it almost never does and put a window in the back. The exhibition caseback shows Calibre 4131, joining the ice-blue Platinum and the Le Mans as the only Daytonas that let you watch it run. There’s platinum in the details too — the bezel ring, the caseback ring, and the tachymeter markings themselves, laid down in PVD platinum on an anthracite Cerachrom bezel and oriented vertically, 12-to-6, a deliberate vintage lean instead of the usual sweep. Forty millimeters of Oystersteel, 100 meters of water resistance, and a Superlative Chronometer rating Rolex just tightened with three new tests for magnetism, reliability, and sustainability.

It’s a chronograph built like it intends to outlast the wrist. Most of them do.

Teddy Baldassarre
