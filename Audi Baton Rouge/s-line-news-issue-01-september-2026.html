On the Road

Next RS6 Avant · Austrian Alps · August 2026 · Carscoops / SH Proshots

Yellow labels, Austrian Alps

In May, a prototype RS6 Avant struck a barrier at the Nürburgring and went dark for a few months. Audi said nothing publicly, which is standard practice. Then in August, photographer SH Proshots found it again — on public roads in the Austrian Alps — and whatever questions had been building since spring came flooding back.

The car was moving through mountain passes wearing full development camouflage, but the body underneath reads clearly enough at camera range. The fenders are wider than the current car by a meaningful margin — not trim adjustments, structural width that signals the RS engineers had room to work. The wheels are a 20-spoke design, large and flat. Ahead of the rear doors, vents cut into the bodywork. Whether those vents are functional heat management for a high-output hybrid system or aesthetic, or both, is not confirmed. The front end remains wrapped. The overall proportion reads as longer, lower, and more planted than the generation it replaces — which is saying something, since the current RS6 is not a subtle object.

The yellow labels are the story in the photographs. They appear on the hood, on the doors, on the trunk. In Europe, yellow hybrid warning labels are a legal requirement — a safety notation for first responders. You cannot opt out of them. They mean this car is electrified in some form.

What they do not mean: anything specific about which engine is underneath. The current RS6 runs a 4.0-liter V8 twin-turbo. Rumors about the next car divide between V6 hybrid and V8 hybrid, with neither side producing documentation that settles it. The figure of approximately 740 horsepower circulates often enough to feel planted, but Audi has not confirmed it. Do not pick a winner.

The debut is spring 2027 — the first public reveal, not a delivery window. For fifteen years the RS6 has come to the United States exclusively as the Avant. Beginning with this generation, Audi will introduce both an Avant and a fastback sedan at the same time.

On the Table

Cocha · 445 N. 6th · Photos: Cocha

The name is a term of endearment

The restaurant has been open since 2016 and people still ask about the name. Cocha is what Enrique Pinerua calls Saskia Spanhoff. A term of endearment, the kind that travels with you, and when you are opening a restaurant together and need to call it something, it becomes that.

Spanhoff is from Baton Rouge originally — LSU, second-generation Dutch. She spent about twenty years on the coast before coming back. She is a certified sommelier, and by the time Cocha opened she had spent something close to twelve years working seriously in wine. The list at the restaurant carries that weight. It is not assembled from distributor sheets. It has a point of view.

Pinerua is Venezuelan. His mother’s family is Basque. That information could be the whole story if the restaurant wanted to be about it. Cocha has never taken that path. When Country Roads wrote about them, the phrase that landed was “cuisine without borders,” which is more accurate than it sounds. The menu is not organized around what is traditional in Venezuela or in the Basque country or in the Netherlands. Those backgrounds are resources to draw from, not constraints to perform within.

The current menu moves through that logic without announcing it. The Spanish octopus arrives with chorizo Bilbao and a white-bean puree — the octopus with enough structure to hold up, the chorizo providing fat and that particular dry-cured Spanish smoke, the beans quieting everything into a coherent bite. The lamb loin comes with a blackberry Shiraz preparation that reads like a dish designed by someone who has thought seriously about what wine does to protein. And then there is cachapa — the Venezuelan corn cake — appearing with the same matter-of-factness as the octopus. No explanation attached. It is just there, and it works.

The chef de cuisine is Nathan McHand. The room is at 445 North Sixth Street, in a stretch of downtown that has been through several iterations and is currently in one of its better ones. Closed Tuesdays. Reservations through Resy.

In September, Cocha will be cooking at Farm Fête. That is a version of the kitchen in a different format. The dinner menu on Sixth Street is the quieter one — the room, the wine list, the plates as they are designed.

On the Town

Farm Fête · Main Street Market · Photos: Cathy Smart / BREADA, 2025

Farm Fête, September 24

BREADA — pronounced “bred-uh” — is the nonprofit that runs the Red Stick Farmers Market, and for one evening in September they stop running a market and throw a party. Farm Fête is the annual fundraiser, and the format has been consistent: chefs, farm food, cocktails, two stages, dancing. This year it is Thursday, September 24, at Main Street Market, from six to nine. Dressy casual. Early tickets are $95; the price moves to $125 as the event approaches.

The full name — Big River Economic and Agricultural Development Alliance — tells you what BREADA is trying to do. The market connects farmers and food producers from fourteen South Louisiana parishes to people in and around Baton Rouge. It started in November 1996 in the parking lot of the Government building on St. Louis Street, out of a master’s thesis by Chris Campany under LSU Landscape Architecture professor Suzanne Turner. BREADA incorporated as a 501(c)(3) the same year. The thesis became infrastructure.

Farm Fête is how BREADA funds the outreach that makes the market more than a Saturday transaction. Chefs cook with what the farms brought. This year Cocha is in the kitchen — Spanhoff and Pinerua’s team, operating at scale, with farm ingredients from the region their restaurant already draws from. It is a different context than dinner on Sixth Street. Same sensibility.

Jason Carter is at Manship the same night. The city has options.

On the Wrist

Omega Speedmaster 38 mm · ref. 324.30.38.50.01.002 · Hodinkee / Omega

Speedmaster 38, reconsidered

Omega has a new generation of the Speedmaster 38 mm, and the most consequential change is also the easiest to miss in a first photograph: the subdials are no longer oval. They are round now, the same shape as the subdials on the 42 mm Moonwatch. Seven variants in the new collection. One is the right choice.

The oval subdials on the previous 38 mm had been the defining point of differentiation — and, depending on your position, the defining problem. The Speedmaster as a line began with the 42 mm case, the chronograph, the watch that went to the Moon. The 38 mm came later, sized for different wrists, and it brought its own subdial geometry that placed visual distance between it and the flagship. The new generation closes that gap. Round subdials, applied Omega logo at twelve, dot-over-ninety tachymeter bezel. The 38 mm now reads from across a table like a Moonwatch, if a smaller one.

The reference to pay attention to is 324.30.38.50.01.002: black dial, no date, $6,500. The case is 38 mm across and 44.96 mm lug to lug. That lug-to-lug number matters as much as the diameter on a smaller wrist. The thickness is 14.75 mm, which is not thin. It is not a dress watch and it is not trying to be.

The movement is Caliber 3332, the no-date automatic. Fifty-two hours of reserve. Water resistance to 100 meters. The bracelet is the five-link with a comfort-release micro-adjust at the clasp — expand it a few millimeters without removing a link. If you have ever adjusted a bracelet mid-flight because the cabin made your wrists swell, you understand why this exists.

Ignore the diamond bezels. This is the clean version. At $6,500 the comparison gets complicated quickly. The Moonwatch starts at $6,100 in the manual-wind version. You are paying a modest premium here for the automatic and the smaller case. Whether that makes sense depends on the wrist and your feelings about winding.

On the Lot

Q3 · Country Club of Louisiana · Audi Baton Rouge

The Q3 that started over

The 2026 Audi Q3 is the third generation of the model and the first complete redesign since 2019. Seven years is a long time in compact SUV math, and Audi did not refresh the car — they rebuilt it. Larger in every dimension. A revised powertrain. An interior that finally matches what you would expect from a brand that prices the entry point accordingly.

On the outside, the difference is visible immediately. Longer and lower in its visual stance, smoother transitions than its predecessor. Car and Driver’s first drive describes a presence that reads as a step up in category rather than a refresh of the same car. S line exterior comes standard on the US version, which means the Q3 arriving here already has the sportier-looking bumpers and sills. OLED taillights are available.

Under the hood: a 2.0-liter turbo four making 255 horsepower and 273 pound-feet. The outgoing car made 228 and 251. Audi quotes 0–60 in 5.5 seconds. Car and Driver’s test of the old Q3 was 7.3. Expect the new one to be meaningfully quicker even if 5.5 involves favorable math. Standard quattro. The transmission is a seven-speed dual-clutch, replacing the eight-speed automatic.

Inside: an 11.9-inch virtual cockpit paired with a 12.8-inch MMI. The 40/20/40 rear seat split gives you more configurability than the outgoing car. Acoustic laminated glass on the front side windows, which sounds minor until you notice it at interstate speed. Starting MSRP from Audi USA is $43,700.

Audi Baton Rouge has thirteen of them in stock and is pricing $2,000 off the all-new model. That is notable because they have not discounted this car before. The Q3 has historically sold at or near price. The new generation is arriving in volume, and ABR is moving on it now.

Q5 · all-new generation · Audi Baton Rouge

The Q5, also started over

The Q5 went through the same kind of restart. New generation. Bigger than the last one. New cabin — a large MMI screen, digital cockpit, the interior language Audi has been moving the whole lineup toward. Mild-hybrid 2.0 and quattro. Car and Driver already called the redesigned Q5 the brand’s bestseller in the United States.

Seventeen in stock at Audi Baton Rouge. Select new Q5s are up to $4,500 off — $2,000 dealer plus a $2,500 rebate.

Q7 · three-row · Audi Baton Rouge

The Q7

The Q7 is the three-row. Not a new-from-zero car the way the Q3 and Q5 are this year. It is the one when more people need to go at once. Twenty-one in stock. Select new Q7s are over $11,000 off — dealer offer stacked with national customer credit.

Oil and filter is $199 if you are booking the shop.
