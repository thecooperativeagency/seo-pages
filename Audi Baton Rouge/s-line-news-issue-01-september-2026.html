September in Baton Rouge is summer that refuses to read the calendar. The heat holds; the light gives back a few minutes a night. Here’s what’s news.

On the Road

Yellow labels, Austrian Alps

Next RS6 Avant · Austrian Alps · August 2026 · Carscoops / SH Proshots

On the Road

Yellow labels, Austrian Alps

The next RS6 went quiet after a prototype clipped a barrier at the Nürburgring in May. In August it turned up again — SH Proshots caught it running public roads in the Austrian Alps, wearing full camouflage and, more usefully, a set of yellow warning labels on the hood, the doors, the trunk.

Those labels are the whole scoop. In Europe they are a legal notice to first responders that a car is electrified. You cannot opt out of them. So this RS6 is a hybrid — that much is now fact.

What they do not tell you is what sits under the electrification. The current car runs a 4.0-liter twin-turbo V8. The next one splits the rumor mill between a V6 hybrid and a V8 hybrid, with a figure near 740 horsepower circulating and nobody able to prove it. We are not picking a side until Audi does.

The body underneath reads clearly enough: fenders visibly wider, big flat 20-spoke wheels, vents cut in ahead of the rear doors. It looks longer and more planted than a car that was never subtle.

The reveal is Spring 2027. And after fifteen years of the RS6 arriving here as the Avant only, this generation brings a fastback sedan alongside the wagon — same platform, same stage.

A Debut, and a Docket

Tiger Stadium · Baton Rouge

On Saturday

A Debut, and a Docket

Clemson comes to Tiger Stadium on Saturday, September 5, and the whole apparatus arrives with it — ESPN’s College GameDay set up on campus, ABC’s cameras, a top-fifteen ranking in both polls. Kickoff is 6:30 p.m. Central. It is also the first time Lane Kiffin will coach a snap for LSU. New man on the sideline, full house, a night game in early September: the building has held all of these before, just never quite in this arrangement.

The other opponent this week wore a robe. A Baton Rouge judge, William Jorden, granted a preliminary injunction barring the NCAA and the SEC from punishing a school for playing certain former NFL players named in a lawsuit — two of whom, Dae’Quan Wright and Zxavian Harris, followed Kiffin over from Ole Miss. The conference answered by suing LSU itself in federal court: the school, its board, president Wade Rousse, athletic director Verge Ausberry, and the coach, over the intent to put those players on the field. The eligibility question is unsettled. The kickoff is not.

So the city gets a debut and a docket in the same weekend, which is a very Louisiana way to open a season. Come for the football; the rest will sort itself out in a courtroom that keeps its own hours. After Clemson, Louisiana Tech comes home the 12th, then Ole Miss in Oxford the 19th, and Texas A&M back in Tiger Stadium the 26th.

On the Table

The name is a term of endearment

Cocha · 445 N. 6th · Photos: Cocha

On the Table

The name is a term of endearment

Cocha has been open since 2016 and the name still gets asked about. It is what Enrique Pinerua calls Saskia Spanhoff. When you open a restaurant together and need to call it something, the private word becomes the sign out front.

Spanhoff is from Baton Rouge — LSU, second-generation Dutch, about twenty years spent on the coast before she came home. She is a certified sommelier with roughly twelve years in wine before the doors opened, and the list reads like it. It has a point of view, not a distributor’s spreadsheet.

Pinerua is Venezuelan; his mother’s family is Basque. That could be the entire pitch. Cocha has never taken the bait. Country Roads landed on “cuisine without borders,” which is more precise than it sounds. The kitchen cooks what interests it and treats those backgrounds as pantry, not costume.

Spanish octopus with chorizo Bilbao and a white-bean puree. Lamb loin with a blackberry Shiraz preparation. And cachapa, the Venezuelan corn cake, on the menu with no explanation and no apology. Chef de cuisine is Nathan McHand.

The room is at 445 N. Sixth Street downtown. Closed Tuesdays. In September the team cooks Farm Fête — the same hands at outdoor scale.

On the Town

Farm Fête, September 24

For one night in September, BREADA — pronounced “bred-uh,” the Big River Economic and Agricultural Development Alliance — stops running a farmers market and throws a party. Farm Fête is the annual fundraiser: chefs, farm food, cocktails, two stages, dancing. Thursday, September 24, at Main Street Market, six to nine. Dressy casual. Early tickets are $95 and climb to $125 as the night nears.

The market it funds opened in November 1996 in the parking lot of the Government building on St. Louis Street, out of a master’s thesis by Chris Campany under LSU landscape architecture professor Suzanne Turner. BREADA incorporated as a nonprofit the same year. Today it links farmers across fourteen South Louisiana parishes to the people who eat what they grow.

This year Cocha is in the kitchen — Pinerua and Spanhoff’s team working farm ingredients from the region their restaurant already draws from, at a scale dinner service never asks of them. Jason Carter is at Manship the same night. The city has options.

On the Wrist

Speedmaster 38, reconsidered

Omega Speedmaster 38 mm · ref. 324.30.38.50.01.002 · Hodinkee / Omega

On the Wrist

Speedmaster 38, reconsidered

Omega has a new generation of the Speedmaster 38 mm, and the change that matters is easy to miss in a first glance: the subdials are round now. For years the 38 wore ovals — the thing that set it apart from the 42 mm Moonwatch, and, for some buyers, the thing that kept them away. Round subdials close that gap. From across a table this now reads as a smaller Moonwatch rather than a different idea.

The one to know is reference 324.30.38.50.01.002: black dial, no date, $6,500. Applied Omega logo at twelve, dot-over-ninety tachymeter bezel, the clean version.

The case is 38 mm across and 44.96 mm lug to lug, so it sits close and low on a smaller wrist. But it is 14.75 mm thick — not thin, and worth saying out loud before you fall for the diameter.

Inside is Caliber 3332, the no-date Master Chronometer automatic: 52-hour reserve, 100 meters of water resistance. The five-link bracelet has a comfort-release micro-adjust at the clasp that lets you gain a few millimeters without pulling a link.

On the Workbench

The lounge, then the work

You don’t wait in a plastic chair under fluorescent tubes. The service lounge at Audi Baton Rouge is a real room — seats, screens, coffee — while factory-trained technicians do the actual job in the bays. Covered drive, so you aren’t standing in the rain handing over keys.

3-in-1 charger · complimentary with every service visit

Every service visit, while supplies last: a 3-in-1 magnetic wireless charger with the store on it. No extra form. You come in. It goes home.

On the board this month: oil and filter for $199. Ten percent off recommended service. $100 off a regular alignment. ACC alignment and recalibration at $699 if the cruise system needs to be set true after glass or front-end work.
