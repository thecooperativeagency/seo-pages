Audi Baton Rouge

Google Ads summary

What we set up, why it shows in Google AI results, and what is actually limiting traffic. Numbers sit under that.

What we did

  • Built Search so Brand, Service, monthly offers, and each model line each have a job.
  • Every month, ads are rewritten from the live specials page — not leftover copy.
  • Stopped paying for junk (competitor / “luxury SUV” research) and stopped service-coupon searches from eating the sales budget.
  • Added a Service Offers campaign from the service-specials page.
  • Turned on Vehicle ads so cars on the lot can show as Google vehicle listings.
  • Built Pre-Owned Q5 Search + Display to the used Q5 page.
  • Turned on AI matching on used Q5 Search, with the landing page locked.
  • Today: same AI matching on new Q5, Q7, and Q8 Search. Google can match extra questions; the click still goes to that model’s shop page. Offer campaigns stay off this, so dollar claims cannot get rewritten onto the wrong page.

Why

  • Spend on people looking for this store, these cars, and these offers — not random research.
  • What Google shows has to match what the website says.
  • Vehicle ads put actual lot inventory in Google results (including AI surfaces).
  • Used Q5, new Q5, Q7, and Q8 are metal we need to move.

AI results

They were already showing. There is no separate “AI ads” campaign to buy. Google AI Overviews and AI Mode pull from Search ads and Vehicle ads. Audi Baton Rouge already had both running, so those ads were already eligible.

Why more of them will show now. AI matching is on for used Q5 and for new Q5 / Q7 / Q8 Search. Those ads can appear on longer, conversational questions people type into AI search. Google still sends the click to that model’s shop page — we did not let it pick a different URL. Vehicle ads keep feeding inventory cards. Offer campaigns stay off this setting.

What actually limits traffic

A daily budget is a ceiling, not a promise. If we say “spend up to $50 a day,” two different things can still hold the campaign down:

  • Budget cap — enough people are searching, but we told Google to stop at $X. We miss some of those auctions on purpose.
  • Volume cap — there are not enough searches for that model in this market to fill the budget. The campaign can underspend even at $50/day. That is demand, not a broken campaign. Raising the daily does not create shoppers who are not searching.

AI matching can catch extra phrasings. It cannot invent search volume.
