On the Road

The First-Ever Audi SQ3, Caught Testing

On the Road

The First-Ever Audi SQ3, Caught Testing

The RS Q3 is dead and never crossed the Atlantic, so America has waited a long time for a fast small Audi SUV. The spy shots say the wait is nearly over — and the car that answers is new. Not an RS revival. An SQ3, the S badge finally landing on the smallest crossover, photographed in gloss black with the grille mesh of the next-generation Q3 and twin pairs of round tailpipes in the S3 pattern.

What's under it is the interesting part, and here we're guessing honestly: the EA888 four-cylinder in roughly 329-horsepower tune, Quattro, a seven-speed dual-clutch — the Golf R and Cupra Formentor family of hardware. Call that an educated guess, not a spec sheet.

What it would mean is simpler: a Q3 you'd choose with your pulse instead of your budget, aimed squarely at the X1 M35i that's had this corner of the lot to itself. Spy photos today. The real reveal, soon.

On the Table

The Hallelujah Crab at Juban's

Juban's has been doing Creole on Perkins long enough to have nothing to prove, which is exactly why it's worth your Tuesday. Jacket-on room, real tablecloths, a kitchen that treats the Gulf like the pantry it is. Closed Mondays — plan around it.

Order the Hallelujah Crab. Crispy soft-shell crab over a seafood stuffing, a Gruyère pomme purée underneath doing quiet, buttery work, creolaise over the top. If you want fish instead, the Redfish Adrian is the grown-up order. Open the table with the Chicken, Duck and Andouille Gumbo. This is the seated dinner before a Manship night. Two people, an unhurried hour. Book it on Tock.

In the Cup

The Chocolate Cappuccino at Highland Coffees

Since 1989, Highland Coffees has held the corner at the North Gates, roasting in-house the whole time. A courtyard out back, a room that runs on regulars, and not a trace of chain theater anywhere in it.

Order the Chocolate Cappuccino — espresso, steamed milk, chocolate, exactly as the menu writes it. Cold instead? The iced coffee cold drip comes in ten varieties a day. Take either to the courtyard and let the morning run long before you point the car up Highland Road.

Gallet Multichron Pilot · Hodinkee

On the Wrist

The Gallet Multichron Pilot Comes Back

Some watches you can admire and never buy. This isn't one of them. Gallet — revived under Breitling's House of Brands — has brought back the 1969 Multichron Pilot, and the point of it is that you can walk in and get one. No waitlist, no gray-market tax, no performance about scarcity. Just a 42mm steel pilot's chronograph with a tri-compax dial, exactly the watch the name promises.

The details are the fun part. A bi-directional 12-hour bezel for a second time zone. A reverse-panda face in black and silver, or a denim-blue dial if you want to lean into the moment. Inside, the Gallet 23 — a worked Sellita SW510 — holding 62 hours in reserve. It reads $4,400 on a strap, $4,600 on the bracelet. Fresh out of Geneva Watch Days, and it's not a Speedmaster retread or a clearance-bin Breitling. It's a pilot chrono you can actually own, which these days is the rarest spec of all.
