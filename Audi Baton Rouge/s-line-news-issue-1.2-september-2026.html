On the Road

The A5 and Q5 got smarter without getting redrawn

A5 · Q5 · Audi of America

On the Road

The A5 and Q5 got smarter without getting redrawn

Not every improvement shows up in the sheet metal. For 2026, Audi reworked the brains of the A5 and Q5 — a genuinely different experience from the driver’s seat. The MMI touch display trades long lists for graphics, dims the busywork, and finally makes the phone feel like part of the car instead of a guest.

The cockpit gets three views you flick through with a wheel-mounted button. The passenger display grew up — Bluetooth headphones, a controller, privacy mode while the driver keeps cabin audio. None of it is a redesign. All of it is why the newest Q5 on the lot drives like a newer idea than its shape lets on.

On the Wrist

The Tudor Black Bay 54, which wastes nothing

Tudor Black Bay 54 · Hodinkee

The Tudor Black Bay 54, which wastes nothing

Everything in watchmaking got bigger for a decade. Tudor went the other way. The Black Bay 54 (ref. 79000) is 37mm across, 11.24mm thick, 46mm lug to lug — a dive watch that sits low and flat under a cuff and still holds 200 meters. No-date black dial with gilt-effect print, in-house MT5400, COSC, 70-hour reserve. About $3,850 on steel bracelet.

Hodinkee, James Stacey. Not last issue’s Speedmaster 38.
