The all-new X5

Meet the fifth-generation X5

October 2026: the first new X5 went on sale. First quarter of 2027: three more follow. Later in 2027: a V8 M Performance model.

This is the fifth generation of the X5, the SUV that started the Sports Activity Vehicle segment in 1999. BMW still builds it in Spartanburg, South Carolina. It now comes with more kinds of drive systems than any BMW before it: gas with a mild hybrid, a plug-in hybrid and, for the first time, a fully electric X5.

The first to go on sale, in October, is the X5 40 xDrive. It has an updated 3.0-liter inline-six with a 48-volt mild hybrid. BMW rates it at 394 horsepower and 428 lb-ft, with 0–60 mph in 5.1 seconds.

The three coming in early 2027 are the rear-wheel-drive X5 40, the X5 50e xDrive plug-in hybrid at 483 horsepower of combined system output and the iX5 60 xDrive. The iX5 is the first all-electric vehicle built at Plant Spartanburg. BMW estimates 570 horsepower and up to 435 miles of range (preliminary, based on EPA test procedures). At a DC fast charger it goes from 10 to 80 percent in about 22 minutes.

BMW USA's published base MSRPs: X5 40, $69,800. X5 40 xDrive, $72,100. X5 50e xDrive, $77,500. iX5 60 xDrive, $79,800. Add $1,450 for destination and handling. These are BMW's list prices, not a BMW of Jackson offer. Come see us on High Street and we'll help you pick the one that fits the way you drive.

On Heritage

Forty years of the M3

The first M3 was not a love letter. It was paperwork. To homologate the race car, BMW had to build at least 5,000 road cars within twelve consecutive months, and in 1987 all 5,000 lined up in Munich for a family photo.

What came out was a boxy E30 with flared arches for fenders and a 2.3-liter four-cylinder, the S14, making 195 horsepower with a catalytic converter. Not a big number. Demand kept the line going: by the end of 1991, 17,970 built, 786 of them convertibles.

Six generations later, the one-millionth BMW M rolled out of Munich this fall, and it was an M3 Competition, in BMW Individual Fire Orange III. Munich builds the first all-electric M3 from 2027. The badge is about to change fuel.

The BMW iX4, an electric SUV coupe

BMW iX4 50 xDrive · Neue Klasse

On the Road

The BMW iX4, an electric SUV coupe

The iX4 is the next Neue Klasse car, with a roofline about an inch and a half lower than the iX3's. The practical one stays practical. This one gets to be a little vain.

It earns it. The 50 xDrive makes 463 horsepower and 0–60 in 4.7 seconds, with an estimated 384 to 428 miles of range. On an 800-volt system it takes up to 400 kW, which BMW says is good for 163 to 184 miles in ten minutes.

The M Sport package is standard, the panoramic roof runs all the way back, and the windshield becomes the gauge cluster with Panoramic Vision. It will tow 4,400 pounds, which nobody will believe until you hitch up the boat.

Sacred Ground Barbecue · 1052 Pocahontas Road

On the Table

Sacred Ground, a Bib Gourmand pick

Just outside Jackson, in Pocahontas, there's a barbecue joint that just got its Bib Gourmand from the Michelin Guide's American South edition. Derek Emerson runs it. He built his own 1,000-gallon offset smoker and lights it at five every morning.

Pecan, red oak, hickory. Fourteen to eighteen hours of fire, every day they're open. Get the prime brisket, 100% prime beef. Add the St. Louis ribs, glazed with their red sauce, cider vinegar and brown sugar. If the "dino bone" beef rib is on, the table gets one. That's not a suggestion.

Thursday through Saturday from 11, Sunday 11 to 3. Closed Monday through Wednesday. They cook until they run out, so the late lunch is a gamble. Come early and stay for shuffleboard. The mailing address still says Jackson.

The oil-filled Ressence Type 10

Ressence Type 10 DN "Dark Night" · Hodinkee

On the Wrist

The oil-filled Ressence Type 10

Most watches show you the time. A Ressence shows you a dial that looks printed on the underside of the crystal, because the whole top half of the watch is full of oil. No hands in the usual sense. No numerals at all on this one.

The Type 10 DN is the first oil-filled Ressence with the brand's own movement, the automatic RW-04, 60 hours of reserve. The two halves of the watch never touch: a titanium membrane separates them, and magnets carry the motion across. It shows hours, minutes, a second time zone, an oil-temperature indicator and a 120-second runner. The case is 41.5mm of titanium with a dark blue coating, water-resistant to 5 atmospheres (ATM). 100 pieces, CHF 45,000, available in November.

It's engineering you can see and can't quite explain to your friends. Which is a pretty good description of a BMW with the right options.
