September in Jackson doesn’t cool off so much as it stops apologizing. The light goes long, the patios stop being a dare, and the calendar fills up with people who actually play instruments. Here’s what’s news.

On the Road

i3 Touring prototype · Nürburgring

The i3 Touring

There’s a photographer named Baldauf who stands at the Nürburgring in the cold and waits for BMW to make a mistake in public. In August he caught one worth caring about: an i3 Touring — the long-roof version of the new Neue Klasse i3 — running laps in a thin coat of camouflage. Silver. Estate body. A wagon that plugs in.

The tester wearing the camo is the dual-motor 50 xDrive: 463 horsepower, a 108.7 kWh pack, something north of 500 miles on the European cycle, and DC charging quick enough that a coffee is a real strategy again. BMW already started building the sedan in Munich. The wagon is the driver’s argument against the assumption that the only honest answer anymore is another tall SUV.

Two things this is not. It is not a hybrid, and it is not confirmed for anybody’s driveway on this side of the Atlantic. A prototype at a German track proves engineers are working, nothing else — not a Mississippi delivery date, not a price, not a promise. So we watch.

On the Table

Elvie’s · 809 Manship St. · Belhaven

Elvie’s

Elvie’s sits in a small room in Belhaven on Manship Street, named for the chef’s grandmother, May Elvaretta Good, and it has spent the years since 2020 quietly being one of the best reasons to stay in Jackson for dinner. Hunter Evans — a James Beard Best Chef South finalist — and Cody McCain cook Mississippi through a French lens without ever pretending the Gulf is somewhere fancier than it is. Bib Gourmand last year. On the New York Times list. None of that is why you go.

You go because September is finally when the patio makes sense, and because the plate in front of you came off a farm and out of the Gulf. As of the live night menu: oysters to start, chicken liver pâté with sourdough and pickles, steak frites finished with maître d’ butter and duck-fat fries. The menu moves with the farms, so check the night page the week you go.

It’s a two-person room. Leave the laptop in the car. Closed Sunday and Monday. Book it before a Duling Hall night, or instead of defaulting to another steakhouse.

On the Town

Cyril Neville · Duling Hall · 24 September

Cyril Neville Plays The Dead

Thursday, 24 September, Duling Hall. Cyril Neville is the youngest Neville brother, which at this point means a Grammy Lifetime Achievement, the Meters, the Neville Brothers, and roughly half a century of New Orleans low end. He is bringing all of that to a 500-seat room in Fondren to play the Grateful Dead. That is not a touring jukebox musical rolling through on a bus. That is a serious musician taking apart songs he loves in a room small enough to hear him think.

Doors 6:30, show 7:30, all ages. Advance $40, day-of $45 plus fees. Under-21 is $5 at the door. Eat at Elvie’s first, or oysters at Saltine if you’re already on Duling.

On the Wrist

Speedmaster 38 · black no-date

Speedmaster 38

Omega redrew the Speedmaster 38 this year and, for once, the news is what they took away. The oval subdials are gone. What’s left, in the black no-date (ref. 324.30.38.50.01.002), is the Moonwatch face on a smaller, more honest case — $6,500, and the one to want out of the seven variants. Ignore the diamond bezels.

It disappears on your wrist until somebody asks about it. Not a smartwatch. Not a $75,000 bracelet that lives in a safe. Still 14.75 mm thick, which means it wears like a tool. The good version of a thing is the one you actually use.
